How pricing works.

Hero’s prices by volume: how much of the truck your load fills. We have not published a rate card because the honest number depends on the pile in front of us, not a formula. Here is exactly how we get to it.

The short version

You are paying for space on the trailer.

Junk removal is priced by volume, not by weight and not by the hour. The number that matters is how much of the trailer your stuff takes up. A single chair takes up almost nothing. A garage takes up most of the trailer. The price scales with that space.

One cubic yard, the base unit we measure against, is roughly the size of a washer or dryer. From there we work in eighth-of-a-load increments, so a quote can reflect a quarter load, three eighths, half, and so on, instead of rounding a small job up to a big one.

Why we quote from a photo instead of guessing

Plenty of companies in this category will not give any number until a truck is already in your driveway. That is exactly the moment a homeowner has the least room to negotiate. We ask for a photo first so the load-size conversation happens before anyone has driven out, when you can still say no with nothing lost.

We are not able to publish a flat rate card, because two garages that look similar can differ by a full eighth of a load once we are sizing the actual items. What we can promise is that the size we quote from your photo is the size we hold to, and if we get to your house and the pile is genuinely bigger than what the photo showed, you hear that before we touch anything.

What affects the load size

  • How much you have. One appliance versus a full garage is the biggest factor by far.
  • How it stacks. Bulky, irregular shapes like mattresses or hot tubs take up more trailer space than their weight suggests.
  • Access. Stairs, tight hallways, or a long carry do not change the load size, but they are worth mentioning in your text so nothing is a surprise.

Questions people ask first

Straight answers.

Why don’t you list prices on the site?
Because an honest number depends on the actual pile, not a formula. A rate card would either be too vague to trust or wrong for most jobs. A photo gets you a real number instead.
How big is a cubic yard, really?
About the size of a washer or dryer. That is the unit we measure every load against.
What is an eighth of a load?
We break the trailer into eighths so a small job is not rounded up to a big one. A couch and a few boxes might be an eighth or a quarter, not a full load.
Can the price change once you arrive?
The goal is no. If what we find is genuinely different from the photo, we tell you before anything is loaded, not after.
Do you charge by the hour?
No. Pricing is based on load size, not time on site.
