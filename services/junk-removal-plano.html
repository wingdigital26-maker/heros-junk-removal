What a Plano house actually produces

Plano is not a new city, and that shows up in the work. Much of it built out through the 1970s and 1980s, so a large share of the housing stock is now four decades old and has been lived in by the same family for a long stretch of that. What we get called for east of Central and around Historic Downtown Plano is different from what we get called for up near the Legacy corridor, where the houses are newer and the calls are more often a remodel or a move.

The three jobs Plano generates more than anywhere else we work:

  • Garages that have absorbed thirty years. A long-owned house produces a garage nobody has fully emptied since they moved in. That is its own service for a reason, and it is covered under garage cleanouts.
  • Estates and downsizings. Older neighborhoods mean families handling a parent's house. That work is quiet, unhurried, and handled under estate cleanouts. Humor stays out of it.
  • Backyard teardowns. Hot tubs, sheds, playsets, and decks that came with a house in 1994 and have outlived the kids who used them. Hot tub removal covers how those actually come out.

Alleys, driveways, and how we get to the load

A lot of the older Plano neighborhoods are set up for alley collection rather than curb, which is a small detail that changes the day. It can be an advantage, since an alley often puts the trailer closer to a back gate than a driveway would. It can also be a problem if the alley is narrow, blocked, or the gate has been painted shut since the last time anyone opened it.

Tell us which side of the house is easier to reach when you write. That one line removes most of the surprises on the day, and it is the single most useful thing a Plano customer can mention up front.

Use the city first when the city will do it free

Plano runs its own programs and they are genuinely good ones. If your load fits inside them, use them. We would rather tell you the truth and lose the small job than take money you did not have to spend.

  • Bulky waste collection handles large residential items on a schedule, with its own limits and accepted list. Read the list before you stack anything out back, because a pile the city declines just sits there.
  • Household chemical collection takes paint, stain, solvents, motor oil, gasoline, pool chemicals, pesticides, and propane tanks from Plano residents at no cost. We do not take any of that, and neither does any other licensed hauler.
  • Residential recycling covers the ordinary weekly stream. Plano is a member city of the North Texas Municipal Water District, which runs the regional solid waste system the city's material flows into.

Where the city stops working is volume and timing. Bulky pickup runs on a schedule and has limits, so a whole garage, a full estate, or anything with a closing date attached is where we come in. The statewide rules behind all of it, including what counts as household hazardous waste, sit with the TCEQ.

Offices and small commercial in the Legacy corridor

Plano carries a lot of office space, and a suite that is closing or downsizing is a cleanout like any other, with a lease date instead of a Saturday. Desks, chairs, cubicle frames, break room appliances, and old electronics all come out the same way a house does. Two things have to be handled before we arrive: paper with personal information on it, and anything holding data. The details are in office and small commercial cleanouts.
