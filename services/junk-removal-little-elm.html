What a Little Elm job actually looks like

Little Elm sits on Lake Lewisville, and that shows up in the calls we get that no inland city sends our way: an old dock box that has taken on water, a patio set that spent one too many summers by the shoreline, or fence and deck sections torn up after a storm off the lake. The older streets closer to the original town center carry a different mix, houses that have been lived in for years with a garage or shed that has been filling the whole time, while the newer additions further from the water send us more standard move-in and remodel loads.

The jobs Little Elm generates more than most other towns we work:

  • Lakeside patio and dock cleanouts. Weathered furniture, dock boxes, and storm-damaged deck material. That overlaps with yard debris.
  • Older garages and sheds. Years of accumulation in the original lake-area neighborhoods. That is covered under garage cleanouts.
  • Everyday moves and remodels. Furniture and appliances swapped out ahead of a sale or an upgrade, covered under furniture removal and appliance removal.

Lake access, older streets, one thing to flag

Getting close to the water in Little Elm can mean a narrower street or a shared drive near the marina and beach park areas, so the crew checks the approach before backing a trailer in rather than guessing. Away from the lake, the streets are standard width and access is rarely the issue. A lot of Little Elm's newer sections carry HOA rules about how long anything can sit at the curb, so we plan to load and leave the same visit. If there is dock or waterfront access involved, tell us when you write and we will plan around it.

Use the town first when it will do it free

Little Elm runs residential trash, recycling, and bulk collection on a schedule, and if your load fits inside it, use it. We would rather tell you the truth and lose the small job than take money you did not have to spend.

  • Paint, stain, solvents, motor oil, gasoline, pool or lake chemicals, pesticides, and propane tanks never ride on our trailer, and no licensed hauler can take them. Those belong in the Town's own household hazardous waste drop-site, not this pickup.

Where the town's own service stops working is volume and timing. Bulk pickup runs on a schedule with limits, so a whole garage, a full house, or anything with a closing date attached is where we come in. The statewide rules behind solid waste handling sit with the TCEQ.
