The short answer

Remodel leftovers, hauled out of the room they are sitting in

Hero’s clears the debris a renovation actually produces: kitchen cabinets and countertops, drywall and trim, doors and windows, carpet and pad, laminate and hardwood, subfloor, old fixtures, cut lumber, and the packaging that shows up with every new appliance. A remodel produces more material than most people expect, and the EPA's guidance on construction and demolition materials is a good plain read on why so much of it can be recycled rather than buried. You do not have to drag it to the curb first. Our crew works inside the house, up the stairs, and out to the driveway pile.

What we will and will not take from a job site

  • Standard demo debris: cabinets, counters, drywall, trim, doors, windows, flooring, subfloor, fixtures, lumber, and packaging. All routine.
  • Dense material: concrete, brick, tile, dirt, and shingles have to be handled differently from ordinary household junk. We look at those before booking, which is the same rule every hauler works under.
  • Household hazardous waste: paint, stain, solvents, and asbestos never go on the trailer. No licensed hauler can take them. Dallas residents use the Home Chemical Collection Center, Plano runs its own household chemical collection, and the TCEQ household hazardous waste guide covers the rest of Texas. Ask us and we will point you at your city’s page.

How a job-site pickup runs

  • You send photos or we come look, and you get a number before anything is lifted. Disposal is inside that number, so the quote is the bill.
  • The crew loads from wherever the debris sits, including a second-floor bathroom or a back bedroom.
  • The space gets swept before we leave, ready for the next trade to start.
  • Metal and cardboard go to recyclers. Usable fixtures and cabinets go to local donation partners such as the Dallas Area Habitat ReStore when they are in shape for it.

Frequently asked questions

What construction debris do you haul?

Cabinets, countertops, drywall, trim, doors and windows, carpet and pad, flooring, subfloor, fixtures, lumber, and remodel packaging. We load it from inside the house or from the driveway pile.

Do you take concrete, brick, tile, and dirt?

Sometimes, and it is handled differently. Dense material has to be handled differently from ordinary household junk, so we look at it before booking.

Can you come while the job is still running?

Yes. Plenty of contractors have us clear one phase of demo before the next trade arrives, then again at the end. Pickups run seven days a week.

Do you take paint and old chemicals from a remodel?

No. Paint, stain, solvents, and asbestos are household hazardous waste, and no licensed hauler can take them. Your city’s household chemical collection center accepts them from residents for free.

Do you clean up after loading?

Yes. The room or driveway is left broom clean. Metal and cardboard from the load go to recyclers rather than straight to a landfill.
