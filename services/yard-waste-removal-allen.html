Yard Waste Removal, Allen

A lot of Allen's yard debris calls trace back to landscaping that is now two to three decades old, planted when the neighborhood was brand new and now being pulled out, cut back, or replaced. We load loose piles as they sit, no bagging required, from anywhere on the property.

What a yard waste removal pickup actually involves

Allen filled in mostly during the late 1990s and 2000s, so the trees and foundation shrubs planted with the original build are now mature enough to be a real removal job rather than a quick trim. That shows up as overgrown foundation beds coming out ahead of a repaint, a dead or storm-damaged tree limb pile, or an entire flower bed of landscaping timbers and rock being replaced. None of it needs to be bagged or bundled first; the crew carries or wheelbarrows loose brush, branches, and cut limbs straight to the trailer from the backyard or side yard.

What makes Allen different

Allen is compact and well laid out, so reaching a backyard pile is rarely the obstacle it can be in an older, narrower-lot city; the detail that actually matters here is the HOA. A lot of Allen neighborhoods, including the streets around Twin Creeks, have rules about how long yard debris can sit visible from the street, so we plan to clear the pile the same visit rather than have it wait on a city collection date and risk a notice.

Frequently asked questions

Do you take an old, overgrown landscaping bed in Allen, not just branches?

Does the debris need to be bagged before an Allen pickup?

No. The crew loads loose piles as they sit, from the backyard, side yard, or curb. Bagging speeds loading up but is never required.

It might. Several Allen neighborhoods around Twin Creeks limit how long yard debris can be visible from the street, so we plan to clear the pile in one visit instead of leaving it staged.
