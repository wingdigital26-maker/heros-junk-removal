Clearing a parent's senior living apartment when the move ends

By the Hero’s team. October 2026. A 6 minute read.

When a parent leaves an assisted or independent living apartment, the family usually has a set number of days to empty the unit. Start by asking the front office for the move-out date, the elevator and loading rules, and what the community removes itself. Then pull medicines, paperwork and rented equipment before any furniture is carried out.

This is a different job from the move in. Our post on downsizing a parent into assisted living covers choosing what fits in the new room. This one covers the end of that stay, whether your parent is moving to a higher level of care, moving in with family, or has passed away. The apartment is smaller than a house, but the timeline is usually shorter and the building has rules a house does not.

What to ask the front office first

Before anyone touches a box, get four answers from the community. Most families find these in the residency agreement, and the front office can confirm them.

  • The date the unit has to be empty. Ask whether that is the last day of the month, a set number of days after notice, or something else, and whether charges continue until the keys are turned in.
  • How moves work in the building. Many communities want the elevator reserved, the crew signed in at the front desk, and furniture carried out through a side or service entrance rather than the main lobby. Ask what hours moves are allowed.
  • What belongs to the community. Some apartments come with a bed, a nightstand, blinds or a small fridge that stay. Get that list so nothing the community owns leaves with the rest.
  • What condition the unit has to be in. Some want it broom clean. Some handle the cleaning themselves after the furniture is out.

If a question comes up about your parent's rights as a resident, every state runs a long-term care ombudsman program that advocates for people living in nursing homes and assisted living. The federal Administration for Community Living ombudsman page explains what the program does and links to each state's office.

What the family handles before the crew arrives

Three things come out of the apartment before any furniture does, and none of them go on our truck.

Medicines. Senior apartments often hold more medication than the family expects, some of it prescribed, some of it over the counter, much of it expired. Check with the nursing staff first, since some communities manage and dispose of medications they dispensed. For the rest, the FDA's guide to disposing of unused medicines puts drug take-back options first. Needles, lancets and insulin pens go in a sharps container, and the FDA's page on getting rid of used needles and other sharps covers how.

Paperwork and valuables. Go through every drawer, the closet shelf and the pockets of the coats before anything is packed. Bank statements, insurance cards, the residency agreement itself and anything with a signature belongs with the family. If there are boxes of files, our post on boxes of books, bookcases and old paperwork covers the shred-or-keep pass.

Rented equipment. A hospital bed, a lift chair, a wheelchair or an oxygen concentrator may be rented, not owned. Medicare's page on durable medical equipment coverage notes that some equipment is rented and some is bought, so call the supplier and schedule the return. Rented equipment goes back to the supplier, never to a hauler. Our post on medical equipment after care ends goes through that sort in more detail.

Oxygen is a hard stop. Oxygen tanks and concentrators go back to the supplier that provided them. We do not carry oxygen cylinders, and neither should anyone moving furniture around them. If you are not sure whether a piece of equipment is rented, leave it in the apartment and call the supplier before the pickup day.

What usually fills a senior living apartment

These apartments are small, so most of what is left was chosen carefully when your parent moved in. That makes it harder to let go of, and it also means the list is short.

  • A recliner or lift chair. Often the most used piece in the apartment. Power recliners and lift chairs are unplugged and carried as one piece. Our post on old recliners and power recliners covers how those come out.
  • The bed. A mattress, box spring and frame, and sometimes an adjustable base with a motor. In a Carrollton community, we take the mattress, box spring and frame out of the unit as one pickup, and in Frisco we regularly carry a mattress down from a third-floor unit without sliding it down the stairwell.
  • A loveseat or small sofa. Sized for the apartment, but still heavy in a hallway. We handle a loveseat or sleeper sofa coming out of a Carrollton apartment the same way we would a house, through the route the building allows.
  • A dresser, a nightstand and a small table. Often pieces that came from the family house.
  • The closet. Clothes, shoes, linens and the boxes that were never unpacked.

Keep, give to family, donate, haul

Sort in that order. The family decides first, because once something is gone it does not come back.

  1. Keep. Photographs, letters, jewelry, anything a family member has asked for. Take these home before the pickup day.
  2. Give to family. Furniture someone has claimed should leave with that person, not wait for the crew.
  3. Donate. Clean furniture in good condition can go to local donation partners. Habitat for Humanity's ReStore donation page explains what most ReStores accept, and our post on donating furniture in DFW covers what donation centers turn down.
  4. Haul. Everything else, including the pieces that are worn out, stained or broken.

If the apartment is part of a larger estate, with a house still to clear as well, our estate cleanout checklist covers the whole sequence.

How the pickup runs in a senior community

Tell us the community, the unit number, the move-out date and any building rules the front office gave you. A crew looks at what you have, from photos or in person, and gives you a firm number before anything is loaded. The quote is the bill, and disposal is included. We are affordable and priced fair, based on how much there is to take.

On the day, the crew signs in where the community asks, uses the elevator and entrance the building has set aside, and carries everything out. Nothing is dragged down a hallway where residents are walking. These are our own crews, not gig labor, which matters in a building full of people who live there. When the apartment is empty, we sweep the floor before we leave. Call before noon and same afternoon is typical across the northern suburbs, though the building's own move hours come first. Prosper sits inside our core service area, so a senior apartment in Prosper is one of the quicker runs for our crews when the move-out date is close.

You do not have to be in the apartment while we work if the community allows access. Many families are handling this from out of town, or in the same week as a funeral. When you are ready, tell us about the apartment, or see how our estate cleanouts run.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a family usually have to clear a senior living apartment?

It depends on the residency agreement, so ask the front office. Some communities set a number of days after notice and some run to the end of the month. Ask too whether charges continue until the keys are returned.

Can you take the hospital bed or the oxygen concentrator?

Not if it is rented. Rented medical equipment and all oxygen equipment go back to the supplier. If the family owns a bed or a lift chair outright, we can carry it out with the rest of the furniture.

What should we do with leftover medications?

Ask the nursing staff first, since some communities handle medications they dispensed. For the rest, use a drug take-back option as the FDA recommends. Medications never go out with the furniture.

Do we need to be there on the pickup day?

Not always. If the community allows the crew access, leave a written keep list and clear those items out first. Take anything you are keeping home before the pickup.

Will the community let a crew use the elevator?

Most do, with a reservation. Ask the front office which elevator and entrance to use and what hours moves are allowed, and we will work inside those rules.

Get the date and the building rules first. After that, the rest can go out quietly, through the door the building has set aside.
