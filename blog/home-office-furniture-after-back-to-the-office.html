Clearing out the home office after going back to the office

By the Hero’s team · September 2026 · 7 min read

We haul home office desks, L-shaped and standing desks, office chairs, filing cabinets, bookcases, monitors, printers and old computer towers. Two jobs are yours before we arrive: delete your personal data from anything with a drive, and empty the filing cabinet. Loose batteries stay behind and go to your city's collection program.

A lot of these rooms were set up in a hurry and then used every day for years. Now the job is in an office again, the desk is collecting mail, and the room could be a bedroom, a nursery or a guest room.

What comes out of a home office

The list on one of these jobs is usually longer than people expect, because a home office grew one purchase at a time:

  • The desk, often an L-shape or a corner unit
  • A standing desk with a motorized frame
  • One or two office chairs, plus the chair mat
  • A two-drawer or four-drawer filing cabinet
  • Bookcases and a wall shelf or two
  • Monitors, a docking station and a tangle of cables
  • A printer, a scanner and a shredder that stopped shredding a while ago
  • An old desktop tower and the laptop before the current one
  • Boxes of paper, binders and the packaging the equipment came in

Everything on that list is something we take. The furniture is the easy part. The electronics and the paper are where the preparation matters.

The desk that was built inside the room

Many home office desks are flat-pack furniture that was carried in as a box and assembled in the room. Put together, an L-shaped desk often will not fit back through the doorway it came in through, and some will not make the turn at the top of the stairs.

Our crew handles that on site. They take the desk apart where it can be taken apart, and where the particleboard has already been through one assembly and will not survive a second, they break it down so it leaves in manageable pieces. Old furniture removal, and getting it out of the house goes deeper on the path out, the stairwell and what comes apart cleanly.

A motorized standing desk is heavier than it looks, mostly in the steel frame and the legs. It comes apart at the frame, and the steel goes to a metal recycler.

Before we arrive, do the two jobs only you can do. Delete your personal information from every computer, laptop and drive that is going. Then empty the filing cabinet and shred what needs shredding. We carry things out. We do not go through your files.

Your data, before the computer leaves

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality says it plainly on its electronics recycling page: delete any private information from hard drives before donating, reselling or recycling a computer. The EPA's electronics donation and recycling guidance says the same thing, and adds that batteries should come out of electronics because they may need to be recycled separately.

What that looks like at home:

  • Back up anything you want to keep.
  • Sign out of your accounts on the machine.
  • Use the reset or erase option built into the operating system.
  • For an old tower with a drive you cannot wipe because it will not boot, pull the drive and keep it, or have a computer shop destroy it.

If the computer belongs to your employer, it is not ours to take and probably not yours to throw away. Company laptops, monitors and docking stations usually go back to the company, so check with your IT department before those end up in the pile.

Loose batteries stay with you

Loose batteries are one thing we leave behind. The EPA's page on used household batteries warns that lithium-ion batteries can start fires even when they look dead, and that they should never go in household garbage or recycling bins. Tape the terminals or bag each battery separately.

Your city handles them. McKinney, for example, accepts lithium and rechargeable batteries through its household hazardous waste and e-waste collection, which residents request before noon the business day before their collection day. The actual pickup date can be up to two weeks out, so ask early. Plano runs its own household chemical collection program, and most nearby cities have something similar.

The same goes for anything chemical that drifted into the office closet: spray adhesive, a can of paint from when the room was last done, compressed-air cans. Those belong in the city program, not in our load.

Where the office furniture and electronics go

Solid wood desks, sturdy bookcases and a good office chair can have a second life with local donation partners. Flat-pack desks that have been assembled once, chairs with a broken gas lift and a chair mat with a crack usually cannot, and they are disposed of. Steel filing cabinets and desk frames go to a metal recycler.

Monitors, printers and towers go into the electronics recycling stream, not the landfill. Texas law also requires computer and TV manufacturers to offer consumers a recycling option, which the TCEQ page above links to, so if you only have one old laptop, that route is worth a look before you book anybody. Appliance and electronics disposal in North Texas covers the rest of the electronics rules, and where your junk goes after pickup explains how a real load gets sorted.

Frequently asked questions

Will you take an L-shaped desk that does not fit through the door?

Yes. The crew takes it apart on site, and if the particleboard will not come apart cleanly, they break it down so it leaves in pieces.

Do you wipe the hard drive on my old computer?

No. Deleting your data is your job before we arrive. Use the reset option built into the computer, or pull the drive and keep it if the machine will not start.

Do you take filing cabinets?

Yes, once they are empty. Shred anything with personal information first. A steel filing cabinet goes to a metal recycler.

Can you take my work laptop and monitor?

If they belong to your employer, check with your IT department first. Company equipment usually has to go back to the company.

Do you take printers, monitors and old towers?

Yes. They go into the electronics recycling stream. Pull any loose batteries out first, since those go to your city's collection program.
