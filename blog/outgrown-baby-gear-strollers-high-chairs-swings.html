Outgrown baby gear: strollers, high chairs, swings and playards

By the Hero’s team. October 2026. A 6 minute read.

Outgrown baby gear comes out in one visit. Our crew carries out the strollers, high chairs, swings, bouncers, playards, baby gates and changing tables from wherever they ended up, folded or not, and loads them together. Your part is pulling the batteries, checking car seat dates, and setting aside anything you plan to pass on yourself.

Baby gear arrives one piece at a time and leaves all at once. The swing that rocked a newborn to sleep gets moved to the garage when the baby learns to crawl. The infant car seat gets swapped for a convertible one. The high chair stays until someone is tall enough for a booster, and then it joins the stroller and the playard in the corner of the garage or the back of a closet. A few years later the youngest is in school, and there is a stack of plastic and folded frames that nobody uses.

What counts as baby gear, and what we take

Almost all of it. The items we carry out most often are:

  • Strollers of every kind, from umbrella strollers to jogging strollers and double-wide models, plus the travel system bases that came with them
  • High chairs, including the full-size kind with trays and the convertible models that turned into a toddler chair
  • Swings, bouncers and jumpers, including the doorway jumpers and the stationary activity centers that take up half a living room
  • Playards and travel cribs, folded or still set up
  • Baby gates and play yards, the pressure-mounted kind and the hardware-mounted kind, once they are off the wall
  • Changing tables and dressers with changing toppers, gliders and rockers, and the toddler bed that came after the crib

If the crib and the bunk beds are leaving at the same time, our guide to clearing out cribs, bunk beds and an outgrown kids room covers the bigger furniture in that room. And if the toys are going too, the playroom cleanout guide covers the play kitchens, ride-on cars and bins.

Before anything leaves: three checks

A few minutes at home makes the pickup smoother and keeps the right things out of the pile.

  1. Pull the batteries. Swings, bouncers, mobiles and activity centers often run on batteries, and some have a power adapter tucked under the seat. Take the batteries out and keep them for your city's battery drop-off or household chemical collection.
  2. Check the car seat labels. Every car seat carries a label with the date it was made. The American Academy of Pediatrics' car seat guidance for families says to avoid a used seat whose history you do not know, and never to use one that is too old, has visible cracks, or is missing its label. A seat that fails any of those should not go to another family.
  3. Set aside what you plan to pass on. If a sister, a neighbor or a friend from church is expecting, pull those pieces first and put them somewhere the crew will not mistake for the haul.

Old does not always mean safe to hand down. Federal safety standards for baby products have tightened over the years, and some older gear was built before the current rules existed. A stroller, high chair or playard that sat in the garage for a decade may also have been recalled since you bought it. If you plan to pass a piece on, look up the brand and model first.

What can go to another family

The Consumer Product Safety Commission sets mandatory federal standards for most durable baby products. The rule for carriages and strollers was published in 2014, the one for high chairs was published in 2018, and play yards have their own standard as well. Because the rules have changed, an older piece may not meet today's standard, which is why we do not treat every old stroller as a donation candidate.

Newer, clean pieces with all their parts are the ones with a real chance of a second home. A gate with its hardware, a high chair with its tray and straps, a stroller that still folds and locks. Our guide on donating furniture in DFW explains how the donation side works and what local outlets tend to accept. Anything that cannot be passed on goes to responsible disposal. A worn-out stroller frame is mostly metal and plastic, and it is handled the same way as the rest of the load.

Where the baby gear usually is

Most of the time it is not in the nursery anymore. It is stacked in the garage behind the bikes, folded into a hall closet, pushed under the stairs, or set up in the attic in case there was a next baby. That is the part of the job we handle: the carrying, down stairs, out of the attic, through the garage and out to the trailer.

In Prosper, newer neighborhoods like Windsong Ranch, Star Trail and Whitley Place are full of families a few years past the baby stage, and the garage is where the gear ends up. Prosper does run weekly bulk pickup on your regular collection day, per the town's trash and recycling page, but many of those neighborhoods also carry HOA rules about how long anything can sit visible from the street. That is one reason families there have the garage cleared in Prosper in a single visit rather than staging a pile at the curb for a week.

In The Colony, on the east shore of Lewisville Lake, some streets near the water are tighter than the newer additions, so a note about the driveway or a shared drive helps the crew plan the approach for a pickup in The Colony before backing the trailer in. And if the crib or toddler mattress is going too, we carry the old mattress out of a Frisco bedroom in the same trip as everything else.

Frequently asked questions

Do you take car seats?

Yes. We take car seats along with the rest of the baby gear. A seat that is past its date, has been in a crash, or has lost its label should not go to another family, so those go to disposal rather than donation.

Do the strollers and high chairs need to be folded or taken apart?

No. Fold them if they fold easily, but the crew handles anything that is still set up. Playards and activity centers can stay assembled.

Can you take baby gates that are mounted to the wall?

Yes, once they are off the wall. Pressure-mounted gates come down by hand. For hardware-mounted gates, unscrew them first so the crew is not removing anything attached to the house.

What about the batteries in swings and bouncers?

Take them out before pickup. Batteries belong at your city's battery or household chemical drop-off, not in the back of a haul.

Will you donate the baby gear?

Clean, newer pieces with all their parts go to local donation partners when they qualify. Older gear, recalled items and anything missing parts go to responsible disposal.

Once the batteries are out and the keepers are set aside, the rest of the baby gear is usually gone in a short visit, and the garage or closet gets its space back.
