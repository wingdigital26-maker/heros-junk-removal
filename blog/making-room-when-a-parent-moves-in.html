Making room when an aging parent moves in with you

By the Hero’s team. October 2026. A 6 minute read.

Making room for a parent usually means clearing one bedroom, the path to it, and part of the garage. Our crew carries out the furniture that is leaving, the office or guest room contents, and the overflow that has nowhere else to go, in one visit before the move. Your part is deciding which room becomes theirs and which of their pieces are coming.

A parent moving in is rarely a sudden decision, but the move date often is. A fall, a diagnosis or a lease ending sets the timeline, and a room that has been a home office, a guest room or a place for the treadmill has to become someone's bedroom within a few weeks. At the same time, some of your parent's own furniture is coming with them, which means some of yours has to leave. This is a practical job, and it is usually done during a stressful stretch, so we keep it simple and quiet.

Which room becomes theirs

Most families pick the room that is easiest to live in, not the one that is easiest to empty. A ground floor bedroom avoids the stairs. A room near a bathroom matters more than a bigger room upstairs. If the only ground floor option is the study or the dining room, that is the room that gets cleared.

That choice decides the job. A study holds a desk, a filing cabinet, bookcases and an office chair. A guest room holds a bed and a dresser nobody uses daily. A bonus room might hold exercise equipment and old toys. Once you know which room it is, you know what has to leave. If the room is a home office, our guide on clearing out home office furniture covers the desks and shelving.

What usually has to leave

Across these jobs, the same things tend to go:

  • The furniture in the chosen room, such as a desk, bookcases, a sleeper sofa, a futon or the guest bed
  • Your duplicates of what your parent is bringing, such as a second recliner, a dresser, a kitchen table or a television stand
  • Extra furniture in the paths, such as an accent chair in the hallway, a bench by the door or a side table in a tight spot
  • Garage overflow, so a parent's boxes, a mobility scooter or a second car have somewhere to go
  • Old mattresses, when your parent is bringing their own bed or a new one is being delivered

Decide what your parent is bringing first. It is easier to let go of your own spare dresser once you know theirs is on the moving truck.

Clear the paths, not just the room

The bedroom is only part of it. The route from the bedroom to the bathroom, the kitchen and the front door has to be clear too. The National Institute on Aging's room by room fall prevention guidance recommends keeping floors and walkways free of clutter and taking care with loose rugs. A narrow hallway with a bookcase in it, or a living room arranged around furniture that no longer fits, is the kind of thing that gets moved or removed before the move-in.

Walk the route the way your parent will. Start in the new bedroom and walk to the bathroom, then to the kitchen, then to the door they will use most. Anything you step around, turn sideways for or catch with a hip is a candidate to go. Do the walk with the lights low, as it would be at night.

If a hospital bed or medical equipment is part of the plan, our guide to clearing a room for a hospital bed covers the space that setup needs.

What not to put in the haul

A few things should be handled separately before anything leaves. Old medicines from your parent's house, or from your own cabinets, go to a take-back site; the FDA explains how to dispose of unused medicines. Paint, cleaners and chemicals from the garage go to your city's household chemical collection; the EPA's household hazardous waste page explains why. We do not take those, and we will point you to your city's program.

Furniture that still has life in it can go to another family. Our guide on donating furniture in DFW explains how that works, and anything that cannot be donated goes to responsible disposal.

If you are also looking for support as a caregiver, the federal Eldercare Locator connects families with local agencies on aging and services in their area.

Where these jobs usually happen

In McKinney, older homes near the downtown square have narrow doorways and stairs that were not built for modern furniture, so a sleeper sofa often has to come apart to leave a study that is becoming a bedroom. The crew can take a heavy old sleeper sofa out of a house near the McKinney square in pieces rather than forcing it.

In Carrollton, some blocks have alley access, which can put the trailer right at the back gate. That makes it simpler to carry a sectional out the back to a Carrollton alley when the living room is being rearranged around a parent's own furniture.

In Wylie, many calls come from long-owned houses where the garage has been filling for years, and a parent's boxes or car need room there. We handle making garage space in a long-owned Wylie house in the same visit as the bedroom.

The house your parent is leaving is often its own job. Our guide to downsizing a parent's household covers that side, and the two can be planned together.

The quote is the bill, and disposal is included. We are affordable and priced fair, based on how much there is to take.

Our own crew does the carrying, not gig labor hired off an app that morning, which matters when people are coming into your home during a hard stretch. Point to what goes, and the crew carries it out and sweeps up. Our page on furniture removal covers the larger pieces. Call before noon and same afternoon is typical across the northern suburbs, which helps when the move date is close. When you are ready, tell us what you have.

Frequently asked questions

Should we clear the room before or after our parent's furniture arrives?

Before. Clear the room and the paths first, then bring their furniture in. Trying to do both on the same day usually means pieces sitting in the hallway.

Can you take furniture from more than one room in the same visit?

Yes. Most of these jobs include the chosen bedroom, a few pieces from the living areas and part of the garage, all in one visit.

Do you take old mattresses and bed frames?

Yes. If your parent is bringing their own bed, we take the old guest bed, mattress and box spring with the rest of the load.

Will you take medicines or chemicals from the garage?

No. Medicines go to a take-back site and chemicals go to your city's household chemical collection. We will point you to the right program.

Can you also clear our parent's old house?

Yes. That is usually a separate visit at their address, and we can plan it around the move date.

Once the room is chosen and the paths are clear, the rest is usually one short visit, and the house is ready before your parent arrives.
