Clearing rooms before foundation or plumbing work starts

By the Hero’s team · 2026

Foundation and slab plumbing crews need the work area completely empty, and clearing it is almost never in their contract. That falls to you, usually with a few days notice. The fastest version is to decide what you actually want back in the room, move that, and have the rest hauled instead of paying to shuffle it twice.

The scheduling call is the part people get wrong.

Why the room has to be empty, and how empty

Interior foundation work means the crew is breaking through the slab inside your house. Slab leak repair and re-piping mean the same thing, or opening walls and ceilings to reach the lines. Either way there is a jackhammer, a spoil pile, and a path from the work area to the front door that has to stay clear all day.

Contractors will usually tell you a room needs to be clear. What they mean in practice is wider than most people assume:

  • Furniture out of the room entirely, not pushed to one wall.
  • Flooring lifted where they are cutting, which for carpet means the pad too.
  • Anything hanging on the walls of that room and often the room next to it, because a jackhammer travels.
  • The path from the work area to the exterior door clear enough for two people carrying material.
  • Cabinets and closets in the work area emptied, since the contents will not survive the vibration standing up.

Ask your contractor to walk the actual footprint with you and mark it, rather than agreeing to a room name over the phone. Rooms next to the work often need to be half cleared too, and finding that out on the morning of is how a job gets pushed a day.

What you are really deciding

Most people frame this as deciding what to throw away. The more useful question is what is worth moving twice.

Everything in that room is going somewhere, coming back, or leaving for good. Anything in the first two groups gets handled twice by somebody, and a fair amount of what sits in a room being repaired is not worth that.

The things that usually are not worth moving twice are the ones already on their way out: the sagging sleeper sofa in the back bedroom, the particleboard bookcase that will not survive being carried again, the treadmill nobody has used since the last time it was moved, boxes that have not been opened since a previous house. Particleboard and laminate furniture in particular tend to come apart the second time they get carried, so paying to store it and then paying to move it back rarely lands well.

Anything solid, anything you actually use, and anything with a reason to keep it goes into temporary storage in another room or the garage. The rule of thumb is to put it somewhere the crew has no reason to walk.

If the furniture side of that list is the hard part, old furniture removal covers what comes apart and what does not.

Do the paperwork pass before you clear anything. Filing cabinets, desk drawers, and closet shelves in the work area all get emptied fast under time pressure, and that is exactly when a title or a policy goes into a box that nobody labels. Pull documents out first, put them in one place outside the work zone, and clear the furniture after. Ten minutes here saves a bad afternoon later.

The two things that have to leave before anyone starts

Household chemicals stored in the work area. Garages and utility rooms are where paint, solvents, pool chemicals, and old pesticide live, and those are also the rooms plumbers open up. None of that goes on our truck, and it should not be sitting near a jackhammer either. Most cities in the northern suburbs take it free from residents. Plano runs a household chemical collection program and the neighboring cities run their own. We will point you at yours.

Anything that makes the contractor's job a legal problem. If the house was built before 1978, cutting into painted walls and trim puts the job under the EPA renovation, repair and painting rule, which sets requirements for the contractor doing the disturbing. That is their obligation rather than yours, but it is worth knowing about before the drywall saw comes out, and it is a fair question to ask when you are picking who does the work.

Timing it so you only pay for one haul

The instinct is to clear everything the day before. The better sequence is usually two passes.

Before the crew arrives, get the room down to empty. This is the pass with a deadline and it is the one worth scheduling help for. Call before noon and the same afternoon is typical across the northern suburbs, though typical is the honest word rather than a promise. How that actually works is in same-day junk removal.

After the work is finished, there is a second, smaller pile: the flooring that came up, the carpet and pad, baseboard, cut drywall, and whatever got soaked if the reason for the job was a leak. Who hauls that depends on your contract. Most foundation and plumbing contractors take out their own spoil and material, and quite a few leave your flooring and your damaged furniture. Read that line in the contract before the work starts, because it is a common surprise. When the job started with water under the slab, what comes out is covered in soaked carpet, drywall and furniture after a slab leak.

Clean concrete, brick, and dirt are handled differently from household junk on our end, so tell us what the pile actually is when you write to us. The EPA keeps a useful overview of construction and demolition materials if you want to understand where that stream goes, and Texas has a general recycling starting point at TCEQ recycling. What happens to a load after we take it is covered in where your junk goes after pickup.

If the reason for the repair was a leak

A slab leak changes the order of operations. The water goes first, the repair goes second, and the hauling waits until things have dried out, because loading wet upholstery and carpet pad into a truck and driving it across town just moves the problem.

Soaked carpet pad, particleboard furniture that has wicked water up its legs, and anything upholstered that stayed wet for more than a day or two are generally not coming back. Cabinets made of solid wood often do. The EPA Fix a Leak material is a decent primer on catching the small ones long before they turn into this one.

Frequently asked questions

Will the foundation crew move my furniture?

Almost never, and where they will, it is usually at your risk rather than theirs. Assume clearing the room is on you and read the contract line about it before the work starts.

How far in advance should the room be cleared?

The day before is usually enough, and it leaves room for the crew to find that an adjacent room needs clearing too. Same day works but it puts everything on one clock.

Can you take the old carpet and pad after they cut the slab?

Yes, once it has dried out. Wet carpet pad is worth waiting a day or two on rather than loading it soaked.

Do you haul concrete from the slab cut?

Tell us before you book. Concrete, brick, and dirt are dense material and are handled differently from household junk, so it changes how the job gets planned.

What do I do with the paint and chemicals in the garage?

Those go to your city household chemical collection program, free, rather than on our truck. We will point you at the right one for your city.
